Introduction

Agentic systems are among the fastest-growing directions in AI. This momentum has now reached 3D, where recent agentic systems have shown strong capability in solving 3D reconstruction, setting up simulation environments, and editing 3D graphics. Despite this progress, we still have a limited understanding of why these systems work so well and which questions matter most next. To build that understanding, we will gather researchers in computer vision, computer graphics, multimodal large language models, and robotics to discuss the following topics:

  • Agentic 3D reconstruction and inverse graphics How should agents be designed to recover the geometry, appearance, and motion of a scene from images and videos, and how do they compare with feed-forward methods?
  • Agentic sim-to-real and real-to-sim How can agents build simulation-ready digital twins from real observations, and can they identify the physical parameters needed to close the gap between simulated and real worlds?
  • Agentic 3D editing and generation Should agents edit and create 3D objects and scenes by writing code, operating modeling software, composing generative models, or combining all three?
  • 3D representations and tools for agents Which 3D representations, such as meshes, programs, procedural graphs, and neural fields, make 3D tasks tractable for agents, and what tool interfaces do agents need?
  • Spatial reasoning of VLM agents How well do current agents reason about geometry and physics, and where do they fail?
  • Benchmarks and evaluation What tasks and protocols can measure the capabilities of 3D agents while remaining hard to saturate or game?
  • Efficiency and reliability Can agentic pipelines become cheap and trustworthy enough for practical use, and what role will output verification, feedback, and reinforcement learning play?

Call for papers

The call for papers will open soon. We invite submissions on agentic systems for 3D tasks, including every topic listed in the introduction.

  • We accept short papers of up to 4 pages and long papers of up to 8 pages, excluding references, in the CVPR format.
  • Papers are non-archival and will not appear in the proceedings, so authors can later submit them to archival venues.
  • Recently accepted papers from other venues are welcome for poster presentation.
  • Reviewing is double-blind and runs on OpenReview.
  • Accepted papers are presented in the poster session, and four are selected for spotlight talks.

Organizers

  • Guandao Yang University of Texas at Austin

    Assistant professor of electrical and computer engineering. He works at the intersection of machine learning, computer vision, and computer graphics, with a focus on 3D generation and geometry processing.

  • Chen Geng Stanford University

    PhD candidate in computer science. He works on 4D vision and graphics, with a focus on data-driven modeling of the physical world.

  • Elliott (Shangzhe) Wu University of Cambridge

    Assistant professor in machine intelligence. He develops data-driven methods for modeling the dynamic physical 3D world from images and videos.

  • R. Kenny Jones Stanford University

    Postdoctoral scholar. He studies visual programs, which are symbolic procedures that generate or analyze shapes, scenes, and motion.

  • Yining Hong Stanford University

    Postdoctoral scholar. She builds embodied agents that explore and interact with the 3D physical world, including 3D foundation models and world models.

  • Haiwen (Haven) Feng UC Berkeley

    Postdoctoral scholar. He works on structured world models and on connecting the reasoning of language models to the physical world through graphics code.

  • Zizhang Li Stanford University

    PhD student in computer science. He works on reconstructing 3D structure from images and on creating controllable dynamic 3D and 4D scenes with generative models and physical simulation.

  • Hengyu Liu University of Texas at Austin

    PhD student. He works on coding agents for computer use and visual artifacts.
